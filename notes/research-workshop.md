## 2. Speakers

### Pieter Libin (VUB, Lecture 1 and host of the 15 September exchange)

Tenure-track assistant professor in computer science at the VUB Artificial Intelligence Lab (https://ai.vub.ac.be/team/pieter-libin/).
His stated focus is "artificial intelligence methods to support decision-making in complex real-world settings" with reinforcement learning, realistic simulation models and robust, interpretable policies (https://ai.vub.ac.be/team/pieter-libin/).
He is principal investigator of ANTICIPATE, an FWO project on AI-supported hospital preparedness for pandemics, and of ARBO-RL, an FWO project on reinforcement learning for arbovirus control in Europe (https://ai.vub.ac.be/team/pieter-libin/).
ANTICIPATE runs 2025 to 2029 with FWO funding of 2,417,459 euro across VUB, UZ Brussel and UAntwerp, and links hospital-level epidemiological simulation to AI that learns which preventive measures work (https://www.myscience.be/en/news/wire/anticipate_prepares_hospitals_for_pandemics_using_artificial_intelligence-2026-vub, https://www.uantwerpen.be/en/staff/philippe-beutels/research/).
He was elected to the Flemish Young Academy in 2024 and is vice-president of the Benelux Association for Artificial Intelligence (https://ai.vub.ac.be/team/pieter-libin/).

Relevant papers:
- Reymond et al. 2024, Exploring the Pareto front of multi-objective COVID-19 mitigation policies using reinforcement learning, Expert Systems with Applications, co-authors include Willem, Abrams, Hens and Nowé (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2024.123686, preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05027).
- Cimpean et al. 2026, Evaluating COVID-19 vaccine allocation policies using Bayesian m-top exploration, Scientific Reports, using Thompson sampling over the STRIDE individual-based model (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-40787-x).
- Thibaut et al. 2026, Predictive triage for testing may improve control of a COVID-19 epidemic while reducing testing requirements, Archives of Public Health (https://doi.org/10.1186/s13690-026-01958-4).
- Silue et al. 2026, Hybrid-AIRL: enhancing inverse reinforcement learning with supervised expert guidance, ESANN, co-authored with Lander Willem (https://doi.org/10.14428/esann/2026.es2026-114).
- Libin et al. 2020, Deep reinforcement learning for large-scale epidemic control, PPO on a Great Britain influenza model (https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13676).
- Libin et al. 2018, Bayesian best-arm identification for selecting influenza mitigation strategies (https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06299).

A 2020 VUB press item describes the STRIDE individual-based model combined with reinforcement learning to learn school closure, mask and vaccination strategies (https://www.vub.be/en/news/vub-puts-ai-against-covid-19).
What AI means here: deep and multi-objective reinforcement learning, Bayesian bandits and best-arm identification, and inverse reinforcement learning, all run against individual-based simulators.
No LLM work by Libin was found in Crossref records from 2022 onwards (https://api.crossref.org/works?query.author=Pieter+Libin&filter=from-pub-date:2022-01-01).

## 3. What "AI" means across this group

Across the hosts, AI means machine learning methods coupled to simulation and statistical models, above all reinforcement learning and Bayesian bandits run against the STRIDE individual-based model (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2024.123686, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-40787-x).
The communication strand treats LLMs as objects of audit and chatbots as channels, not as tools for building models (https://doi.org/10.1162/qss.a.502, https://doi.org/10.2196/41148).
No speaker was found to have published on agentic LLM coding assistants, LLM-driven agent-based models or foundation time series models.

Ranked by frequency in the speakers' output:
1. Deep and multi-objective reinforcement learning on individual-based simulators (Libin, Cimpean, Reymond with Abrams, Willem and Hens) (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2024.123686).
2. Bayesian bandits, best-arm identification and m-top exploration for choosing mitigation policies under a simulation budget (Libin, Cimpean) (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-40787-x, https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.06299).
3. Tree-based and spatial machine learning for risk mapping and disease mapping (Dupas; Janssens with Libin) (https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/104748v1, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sste.2024.100654).
4. LLM behaviour audits, GNN detection of LLM output and science of science (Algaba) (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20704).
5. Chatbots and generative AI as communication media (Poels) (https://doi.org/10.2196/41148).
6. Deep learning for images and structures, including RKI's ZKI-PH image analysis unit and Libin's protein structure work (https://www.rki.de/EN/Institute/Organisation/Departments/ZKI-PH/ZKI-PH.html, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-64923-9).

## 5. Implications for Sam's two talks

- The room's working definition of AI is reinforcement learning and bandits on simulators, so a keynote built on agentic LLMs for modelling workflows fills a gap rather than repeating anyone (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2024.123686, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-40787-x).
- Use the Kraemer et al. taxonomy as the shared map and place both the hosts' methods and agentic LLMs on it, since that paper is the reference point this community will recognise (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08564-w).
- Do not explain reinforcement learning, multi-objective optimisation, Bayesian bandits or STRIDE; Abrams and Libin's lecture and Cimpean's case study will cover them from their own papers (https://ai.vub.ac.be/team/pieter-libin/, https://researchportal.vub.be/en/persons/ioana-alexandra-cimpean).
- Do not spend time on machine learning risk mapping or spatial methods, which Dupas will present (https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/104748v1).
- The behaviour gap in the Belgian scoping review is a hook this room already accepts, so LLM-based behavioural agents or LLM-assisted model building can be framed as one answer to their own finding (https://doi.org/10.1186/s13690-026-01959-3).
- For Lecture 2, Algaba will cover LLMs as citation-biased scientific actors and Poels will cover chatbots and message design, so Sam's communication talk should centre on communicating model outputs and uncertainty when AI writes, reviews and summarises science, and on reproducible agent workflows as a communication device (https://doi.org/10.1162/qss.a.502, https://doi.org/10.2196/41148).
- The panel is moderated by a health economist whose current AI project is hospital optimisation, so tying agentic tools to decision-support and evaluation will land with the moderator (https://www.uantwerpen.be/en/staff/philippe-beutels/research/).
- Doctoral School funding and a poster session imply many PhD students in the room, so pitch at a level that assumes statistical modelling but not LLM tooling (https://www.simid.be/events/workshop2026/).
